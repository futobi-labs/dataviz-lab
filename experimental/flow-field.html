Experimental

Wind ink

A surface pressure map, drawn not as isobars but as the wind it would produce. Every streak is a particle carried by the field for a few seconds of its life. The weather is invented; the physics is a reasonable cartoon of the real thing.

Seven pressure systems over a weak westerly

Wind runs along the isobars, counterclockwise around lows and clockwise around highs, leaking about 16 degrees toward low pressure. Hover anywhere for pressure and wind.

Ink: wind speed

What is encoded

  • Direction of every streak is the wind direction at that point: geostrophic flow along the isobars plus a friction term that tilts it toward low pressure. That is why the particles circle rather than drain from H to L.
  • Speed is encoded twice: streak length (each trail is a fixed number of frames, so faster air draws longer) and the sequential ramp. The redundancy is deliberate; length alone is hard to read in a dense field.
  • Particle density means nothing. Seeds are uniform and respawn at random, so bunching is only convergence, not volume.

What it trades away

  • You cannot read a value off the canvas. The isobar overlay and the hover readout are the actual chart; the ink is the mood around it.
  • Calm regions look empty, which reads as "no data" rather than "no wind". A static vector grid (the Arrows toggle) is less beautiful and more honest about that.
  • Trail length is a time exposure. Change it and the same field looks stormier or calmer, with no change in the data.

Data: synthetic. Seven Gaussian pressure centers (amplitude 9 to 22 hPa) placed with Lab.rng(42) over a 1013 hPa base and a 10 hPa north-south background gradient; particle seeds drawn from the same generator. Wind speed is scaled so the steepest gradient on the map is 45 kt.
