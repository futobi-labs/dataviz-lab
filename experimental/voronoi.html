Stained glass

A soil-moisture network rendered as a church window. Each pane is the territory closest to one sensor, glazed by its reading, with the lead lines drawn in the page color. Over two days a rain front crosses from west to east and the glass changes color behind it.

Soil moisture across 216 sensors, hourly for 48 hours

Anomaly against each sensor's 30-day baseline. A dry patch sits in the east before the front arrives. Hover a pane for its sensor and its 48-hour trace.

Panes drawn at true sensor positions.

What is encoded

  • Pane color is the reading, binned to seven classes: anomaly on a diverging ramp around zero, or volumetric water content on a single-hue ramp. Binning is deliberate; continuous glass would invite false precision.
  • Pane area is not data. It is the inverse of sensor density, so the sparse corners get the biggest, loudest panes from the fewest readings. That is the central lie of a Voronoi choropleth, and the window makes it beautiful.
  • The strip below is the honest twin: network-mean anomaly by hour, with no geometry to distort it. Drag it to scrub.

What the relaxation does

  • Lloyd relaxation moves every sensor toward the centroid of its own pane, repeatedly, until the panes are even. It looks calmer and it is wrong: readings are now drawn where no sensor stood. The readout counts the drift; the mesh toggle draws each sensor's displacement.
  • Relaxed glass is how most generative Voronoi art is made. Use it to see how much of the original's character was the sampling pattern, not the moisture.

Data: synthetic, generated in-page with Lab.rng(42). Sensors from a clustered point process (9 parent sites, Gaussian offspring) plus a uniform scatter; baseline water content and static anomaly from smooth Gaussian fields plus noise; hourly series add a diurnal drying cycle, a rain front with a west-to-east arrival between hours 18 and 34, and hourly noise.
