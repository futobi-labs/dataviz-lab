Sunflower sort

Fifteen hundred orders packed the way a sunflower packs seeds: each one turned 137.5 degrees from the last and pushed out by the square root of its position. Sort the list and the flower reorganises. Rank becomes radius, and because area grows with rank, any run of the list occupies an annulus whose area is its count.

Channel mix, center to edge

Ten rings of 150 orders each. Equal counts means equal areas on the flower, so this is the flower read as bars.

What to look at

  • Value: Wholesale fills the core almost alone. Retail and Online interleave outside it, Online drifting toward the rim because its orders run smaller; the flower shows that overlap as speckle rather than as two humps.
  • Date: the spiral becomes growth rings. Online's share climbs toward the rim, and the ring labels bunch at the end: the last quarter of the year's orders arrives after late October, so the outer annulus covers barely two months.
  • Channel: three clean annuli. Their areas are the counts, which is the one comparison the flower makes honestly.
  • Unsorted: the same seeds, now noise. Every pattern above lives in the ordering, none in the marks.

What it trades away

  • Radius is a square-root scale of rank, so the inner rings are compressed: the top 10 percent occupy a tenth of the area but almost a third of the radius. Eyes read distance from the center, not area, and overweight the core.
  • Angle carries no information at all. Neighbouring seeds on screen can be hundreds of ranks apart on different arms, so local clusters are illusory.
  • Value itself is never shown, only its order. A bar chart of sorted values or a strip plot by channel answers every question here faster; the flower is the invitation to ask them.

Data: synthetic, generated in-page with Lab.rng(42). 1,500 fictional orders dated through 2025 with a November to December peak; Online's share rises from 18 to 45 percent over the year; values are lognormal by channel (medians $2,400 Wholesale, $160 Retail, $85 Online).
