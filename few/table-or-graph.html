Table or graph

Show Me the Numbers starts with one question: will the reader look up values, or look for a pattern? Tables serve the first, graphs the second, and most bad reports answer the question wrong. One synthetic dataset, three treatments.

Notes

  • Show Me the Numbers: tables when the reader needs precise individual values or values of different units side by side; graphs when the message is in the shape, the trend, the exceptions, the relationships. The table above is designed for lookup: right-aligned tabular figures, rules only where they group, and a single quiet emphasis for exceptions.
  • Line versus bar is not taste. A line connects values that belong to a continuous sequence, so the slope between points is meaning, and time is the canonical case. Bars emphasize each value as a discrete amount and support comparison within a month. Same data, different question.
  • Few's "Save the Pies for Dessert" makes the case against the pie: we judge angle and area poorly, and judgments get worse as slices approach equal size. Position along a common scale is the most accurate encoding we have, which is what the sorted bar chart uses.
  • Arguable: the pie does one thing the bars do not, which is make part-of-a-whole unmistakable. For two or three slices with a single dominant one, that can be worth the imprecision. Six near-equal slices is never that case.

Data: synthetic, generated in-page with Lab.rng(11) for the monthly series and Lab.rng(5) for channel shares.
