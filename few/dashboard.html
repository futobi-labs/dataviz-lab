One-screen sales dashboard

Few's definition is strict: the most important information needed to achieve one or more objectives, consolidated on a single screen so it can be monitored at a glance. Gray carries the context. Color appears only where something needs a person to act.

Notes

  • Information Dashboard Design's thirteen common mistakes are the checklist here: no scrolling to see the whole, no gauges, no decoration, no color without meaning, no chart that needs a legend to decode. The KPI row is bullet graph plus sparkline plus number, Few's own combination for "where are we, how did we get here, how far from target".
  • Two hues only. Red flags status and always travels with a ▼ and a label, so it survives grayscale printing and color-vision deficiency. Everything else is gray or near-black, which is why the red is noticed in under a second.
  • The region bars are sorted by revenue, not by plan attainment. That keeps the ranking honest about size while the right column and the red flag carry attainment. Sorting by attainment is defensible too; pick the one the reader acts on.
  • The trend line has no zero baseline. Lines encode position and slope, not length, and Few allows the scale to fit the data. Bars on the same page do start at zero.
  • Arguable: prorating an annual plan evenly across months assumes no seasonality, which flatters the first half and punishes the fourth quarter. A real dashboard should use a phased plan.

Data: synthetic, generated in-page with Lab.rng(42) for the KPIs (few/few.js) and Lab.rng(2026) for months, regions and products.
