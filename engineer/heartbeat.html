The engineer's eye

The nightly heartbeat

Everyone builds the dashboard that says whether last night succeeded. Almost nobody looks at a year of nights at once, which is where the slow creep and the incidents live. This warehouse met its 06:00 SLA on of nights. The misses come in two kinds that one SLA flag lumps together: nights Gold was late, and nights it was on time and incomplete.

A year in one strip

When Gold finished, every night for a year

One mark per nightly run. Later is higher. The band is the middle half of a centered four-week window, the line its median. Click a night, or focus the chart and use the arrow keys, to open it below.

One night, task by task

The selected run as a Gantt chart

All 42 tables, grouped by layer. Bronze bars wear their source system's color; Silver and Gold are neutral. Hairlines before a bar are time the table was waiting, and why. The outlined chain is the critical path: the tasks that set the finish.

Where the night goes

The critical path of every night, split three ways

Minutes from 01:00 to the Gold finish, walked back along the critical path. Each night's three bars add up to its run. Waiting on a dependency is not a fourth row: along the path it is simply the upstream table's running time. Same scale in all three rows.

Notes

  • The strip has two ribbons, not one. Sunday and Monday runs load a weekend's business and finish around , against the rest of the week. Average them and you get a typical night that never happens. Under both ribbons the clean nights crept from to in a year: no alert fires for that.
  • A finish time cannot tell a skipped table from a fast one. The first ERP rename night finished at , earlier than the median night, because nothing downstream of items ran. Plot finish time without status and February's worst night reads as a good one. That is why the broken nights wear a different mark, not a different shade.
  • Two tables own the night. fct_inventory_daily or fct_web_sessions finished last on nights. Tuning anything else moves the Gantt, not the SLA.
  • The queue is not the problem it looks like. Summed over all 42 tasks, a median night holds of task time queued for one of four slots. Only of that sits on the critical path. More capacity would empty the queue and barely move 06:00, except in the throttled fortnight, when capacity was the incident.
  • Against all of this: at 4 a.m. the on-call engineer needs one bit, did it land, and a pager delivers it fine. This view is for the Monday after, when somebody asks why 06:00 keeps getting closer and the run history is the only witness.
