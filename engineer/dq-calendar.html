The engineer's eye

A year of tests

Every night the warehouse runs data-quality tests: not null, unique, freshness, volume, relationships. A year of that is results, and the test log shows them as a list nobody reads. Laid out as a calendar, the year has a pulse, a few scars, and one test that has been shouting on schedule since January.

The calendar counts; it does not name. Unroll it by test instead: one row per test that warned or failed at least once, one tick per bad night, sorted by how often. Two stories stand out before you read a single label.

Fix the test, not the data

The General Ledger closes the books in the first three nights of every month and posts about five times its usual journal lines. That is the business working. The volume test compares each night with the median of the same weekday over the prior four weeks, so it sees a quiet Wednesday become a month-end Wednesday and calls it an anomaly, on three tables, three nights running, every month. The fix is a better baseline: compare month-end nights with earlier month-end nights, and keep month-end out of everyone else's baseline.
