Uncertainty you can feel

An error bar is a summary of a distribution that most readers then decode as a range of equally likely values, or ignore. These three figures put the distribution back in front of the reader as frequencies, outcomes and paths, each beside the conventional view it replaces.

Notes

  • The static panel invites the classic mistake. The two 95% intervals overlap, and many readers, researchers included, take overlap to mean no difference (Belia, Fidler, Williams and Cumming, 2005). Under the same model, the probability that B beats A is . The HOPs panel lets you count your way to that number without being told it. Hullman, Resnick and Adar (2015) found exactly this advantage for judging which of two variables is larger.
  • HOPs mislead when the frame rate is wrong or the frame escapes. Too fast and frames blur into a smear; too slow and people anchor on whatever they saw first. A screenshot freezes one random world and presents it as the answer. Frames jump with no tween on purpose: interpolating between draws invents outcomes nobody sampled. Kale, Nguyen, Kay and Hullman (2019) extend the idea to trends.
  • Quantile dotplots turn probability into counting. "Fourteen of twenty" is a frequency, which people reason about better than a density (Kay, Kola, Hullman and Munson, 2016, the "When (ish) is my bus?" study; Fernandes et al., 2018, showed better transit decisions with them). The cost is resolution: twenty dots cannot express a 1-in-100 tail, and the snapped deadline hides that the model's own answer sits between dots.
  • Fans read as walls. The outer band edge gets treated as a bound, the median line gets treated as the forecast, and nothing in a band says how any single future moves week to week. Spaghetti fixes the path problem and creates another: readers over-weight one salient line, as Ruginski et al. (2016) found with hurricane ensembles versus the cone.
  • Pick by the decision, not the aesthetics. A recurring argument in this literature, Correll's especially, is that the right uncertainty display depends on the question. Ranking two options: HOPs. A threshold decision: dotplot. Planning capacity against a range: the fan, with its percentiles labeled.

Source: synthetic. A/B counts are invented, with draws from a normal approximation to each variant's posterior conversion rate. Bus arrival is a log-normal prediction with a median of 11 minutes. Demand history and forecast paths are seeded log-scale random walks with annual seasonality.
